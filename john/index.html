The method

How REVIVAL takes the pressure off your pelvic floor

  1. 1Stop bracing your core

    Let your belly soften. Sucking in and bracing all day pushes the pressure straight down onto your pelvic floor.

  2. 2Breathe with your diaphragm

    Your diaphragm and your pelvic floor move together, two ends of the same pump. Breathe in through your nose, wide and low, and the floor stretches open. If your shoulders rise when you breathe in, it stays locked.

  3. 3Spread your ribs

    Lift and widen your rib cage from the muscles between your ribs, so it stays suspended instead of collapsing down onto your pelvic floor every time you exhale.

  4. 4Let it get long before you squeeze

    A healthy pelvic floor works like a trampoline. It stretches long when pressure comes down and springs back when the pressure leaves. A floor that’s always gripping can’t do either, and squeezing it harder only makes the problem stronger.

  5. 5Support it with your bum

    The line starts under your big toe and runs up through your calf and hamstring into your glutes. When that line is working, your bum helps carry the load and your pelvic floor stops holding everything alone.

Questions

Isn’t the pelvic floor a women’s thing?

No. You have one too. It holds up everything in your lower body, it decides how well you empty, and it has worked every day of your life without one day of training.

Dribbling after you zip up, rushing to find a bathroom, getting up at night to pee, straining on the toilet, and tailbone or low back pain from sitting are all pelvic floor issues. Most men just don’t know that they are.

My doctor told me to do Kegels. Aren’t those good?

Most men think a strong pelvic floor is a tight one. A muscle that never lets go has nothing left to give, the same way a fist you’ve been squeezing for ten minutes has the weakest grip in the room.

A healthy pelvic floor stretches long when pressure comes down and springs back when it leaves. That’s why there are no Kegels in REVIVAL. Your pelvic floor learns to let go first, through your breath, your ribs and your glutes.

I already train. Isn’t that enough?

Training doesn’t fix it on its own. A pelvis pushed into a tucked position takes your glutes offline, so the muscle that’s supposed to support the floor from behind goes flat and does nothing.

That’s how men who train hard still end up with a flat butt and a little belly pooch. REVIVAL changes how you move, so the support comes from the right places.

Does this work for women?

Yes. These principles cover leaking, prolapse, diastasis recti, painful sex, pregnancy and postpartum. If your partner deals with any of these, it’s the same membership.

I’m a beginner, older, or in pain. Can I do this?

Yes. There’s the Foundation series, a Reshape series for doing bodywork on yourself, and a Regulate series where you can work completely on the floor. Move slowly. Pain is never right.

What is the time commitment?

You can make it fit your life. The theme is made of little videos that fit into your daily life, and you can do them at home, anytime.

We recommend three 20 to 60 minute strength workouts each week, 10 to 60 minute videos on the other days, and Sunday rest for sunshine and family.

What equipment will I need?

Most videos require no special equipment, and each one tells you what you need before you start.

A mat or a rug is enough, outside whenever possible. Occasionally you’ll use weights, rocks, or a countertop, and you’ll be shown how to use what you already have.

Should I take the REPATTERN class or do REVIVAL?

REPATTERN class is Dr. Lily’s most in-depth offering, with videos around an hour each, teaching the anatomy, theory, and foundational principles behind this work.

REVIVAL brings those principles into shorter, easier-to-fit sessions with strength training workouts, without as much of the anatomical backstory.

Start with REPATTERN if you want to deeply understand the theory. Choose REVIVAL if you want more digestible chunks of guidance, or have done REPATTERN already.

Is REVIVAL right for my injury or issue?

REVIVAL is not medical treatment and is not intended to diagnose, treat, or cure any medical condition. It is a movement-based practice designed to help guide the body toward a more natural, healthy, open posture.

These principles have also helped people better support their bodies while experiencing concerns such as diastasis recti, leakage, pelvic floor dysfunction, low-back pain, plantar fasciitis, knee and shoulder discomfort, neck pain, headaches, jaw pain, grinding and clenching, airway concerns, scoliosis, and more.

Your health is your responsibility. Reach out to your provider if you need help.

I have hypermobility

This is especially for you. Learning how to hold your joints is good for everyone but vital for you. Dr. Lily is hypermobile herself, and after getting diagnosed and not finding good answers on how to support her body, she dove deep into figuring it out for herself.

Take it slow and use a mirror to see what you’re actually doing.
